Data Filtering

When enabled, identifies brief high-torque spikes at low cadence (< 60 RPM) that exceed a dynamic threshold (median + 1.5 standard deviations from your ride data). These transient spikes—from junction starts, momentary grinding, or single hard pedal strokes—are replaced with your median torque value. This preserves sustained high-torque efforts while removing brief mechanical spikes that don't affect cardiovascular performance, but can cause bulges in the torque chart giving an unrealistic impression of higher torque efforts, especially if there is a cluster of them. Does not affect HR drift analysis.

    Interpreting Your Heart Rate Drift

    0-3% Drift: Excellent

    Your aerobic system is well-developed and efficiently coupled. Your cardiovascular system is handling the workload with minimal stress. This indicates excellent endurance fitness.

    3-5% Drift: Good

    Slight decoupling is occurring but still within acceptable range. Your aerobic system is coping well but showing some signs of fatigue. Continue building your aerobic base.

    5-10% Drift: Moderate

    Noticeable cardiovascular decoupling is present. Your heart is working harder to maintain the same power output. Consider more aerobic base training and ensure adequate recovery between sessions.

    10%+ Drift: Significant

    Substantial decoupling indicates your aerobic system is under considerable stress. This may signal inadequate aerobic fitness for the intensity, insufficient recovery, dehydration, or heat stress. Focus on building aerobic base at lower intensities.

    ✅ DTL (Decoupled Training Load) Formula (v1.10.12 - LT1-based):

    Uses EXPONENTIAL weighting: Σ e^(0.15×(drift%-5%)) - 1 for physiologically accurate stress quantification.
    Based on Banister TRIMP methodology where higher intensities receive exponentially greater weighting.
    Reports TOTAL accumulated DTL for the ride (not normalized per hour).

    📚 Scientific Reference:

    Banister, E.W. (1991) "Modeling Elite Athletic Performance" in Physiological Testing of Elite Athletes, Human Kinetics.
    Establishes precedent for cumulative cardiovascular load quantification via TRIMP (Training Impulse).
    DTL applies similar cumulative stress concept to progressive HR drift rather than absolute HR intensity.

    ℹ️ About HR Drift Analysis

    This tool is designed for Zone 2 endurance rides - steady-state aerobic efforts of 90+ minutes. During prolonged exercise at constant power, heart rate gradually increases due to thermoregulation, plasma volume reduction, and other cardiovascular adaptations. This "drift" is a key indicator of aerobic fitness.

    Interpretation: <5% drift = excellent fitness | 5-10% = good | >10% = needs work or intensity too high

    Based on research by Coyle & González-Alonso (2001) on cardiovascular drift during prolonged exercise.
